# Rules lawyer: table-play coverage

The TXT files are the rules authority. Generated JSON is reference and typed rules data; the Python tools are opt-in consumers, not a complete tabletop engine. A rule appearing in JSON does not mean the sheet or encounter enforces it.

**DM authority:** Automation applies the default rules and handles bookkeeping; it never takes precedence over a DM ruling. The DM may supply missing facts, override a calculated result, skip or replace a transition, or adjudicate the outcome manually, even when the rule is fully typed. Keep encounter-specific rulings in encounter or character state rather than rewriting canon. This is a design requirement for future integrations, not a claim that every current helper exposes an override API.

When closing a gap, update the relevant TXT first if the rule changes, regenerate `dnd-core.json`, and add a test for the behavior rather than just checking that a record exists.

### Standalone template catalogue

`dnd35_templates.json` now includes normalized `acquisitionModes` (`null` means unspecified), `formOf` links for lycanthrope form records, and `requiresTemplateId` links for the Ghost, Skeleton, Vampire and Zombie variant families. A parent link is not the full eligibility rule; read the original entry for additional restrictions.

`coreV2Reference.effectiveContributions` uses the field names and value shapes of `progression.races[].effective` in `dnd-core.json`: `abilityAdjustments`, `darkvisionFeet`, `energyResistance`, and `levelAdjustment`. These are contributions, not a complete replacement race profile. When integration is enabled, sum adjustments and LA, use the greater darkvision range and resistance per damage type, and never treat LA as Hit Dice. `sourceLines` points to one-based lines in `stats.raw` (arrays track the corresponding array entries).

Fixed and absent ability scores have no equivalent in the current race model. They remain explicit under `unsupportedAbilityChanges`, not disguised as adjustments or zeros. Conditional effects, HD tables and transformation costs remain reference-only. Run `python tools/normalize_templates.py` after editing source stat lines and `python tools/normalize_templates.py --check` to check for stale derived data.

### Template implementation checklist

| Area | Requirement | Catalogue example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hit Dice and progression | Distinguish adding racial HD, changing die size, and recalculating BAB or saves. Keep HD, class levels, LA, and CR independent. A type change alone must not recalculate everything. | Lycanthropy adds the animal's HD, BAB, and base saves; Arachnoid changes type but keeps BAB and base saves. |
| Forms versus persistent changes | Layer persistent template overrides over the base creature, then apply the active form's overrides. Keep original creature and base-animal inputs available. A form switch must not reapply persistent grants or reset HP, equipment, or ability uses. Apply any explicit form-specific gear or HP rules separately. | Lycanthrope animal and hybrid forms read the base animal's physical scores; Ghost's AC depends on manifestation. |
| Replacing or suppressing features | Target specific grants by source, so losing one grant doesn't erase a feature independently granted elsewhere. Preserve the original build in history even if all or part of it is currently unusable. | Psychic Vampire replaces Energy Drain; Zombie loses class levels, skills, and feats in the catalogue. |
| Acquisition and removal | Check eligibility and costs at the right time and record them once. Rebuilding the sheet must not re-charge XP or reroll choices. Removal can have its own consequences instead of simply undoing acquisition. | Necropolitan's ritual costs one level and XP; Mirage Mullah can lose its template after leaving the oasis. |
| Conditional rules and traits | Distinguish unconditional changes from effects that depend on current context, and identify what they override. | Ghost retains natural armor on the Ethereal Plane but changes defenses when manifesting; Vampire sunlight exposure has consequences across rounds. |
| Multiple templates and order | Evaluate eligibility at application time against the appropriate creature state. Represent dependencies, repeatability, and removal explicitly; don't assume overrides commute. | Evolved can repeat; Sanctified removes Fiendish if present. |

**Proposed override model:** base creature → persistent template overrides → active form overrides → temporary effects for the current situation. An override can add, replace, or suppress; it need not be a numeric bonus. For mutually exclusive forms, store one active form ID per form group, not independent switches that can activate incompatible forms. Derive the current sheet from the active layers; switching forms changes the selection, not the base record. Store acquisition and removal events, choices, current form, HP, equipment, and spent uses in character state, not in the canon definition. Costs and other one-time consequences belong in those events, not in the recalculation.

**Table decisions before implementation:**

1. **Lycanthropy:** Decide what happens to gear, spellcasting, and character control when switching forms, and what persists after switching back or curing the affliction.
2. **Undead:** Decide whether the player keeps control after a transformation that removes class levels or features, and which parts of the original build return if the creature is restored to life.
3. **Mid-play transformations:** Decide what requires DM confirmation, especially changes to XP, alignment, class abilities, or player control.

The catalogue records this distinction in `overrideModel` and each entry's `overrideLayer`. The three lycanthrope forms share one `formGroupId`; the common template is persistent. Listing-only entries have no assigned layer. Some persistent templates describe form changes in prose; those forms are not separate typed records yet.

The catalogue audit splits template-specific alternate shapes, manifestations, and possession modes into reference-only `formProfiles`; their owning templates remain persistent. `sourceEffectIndexes` are zero-based indices into the owning entry's `other_effects`. Vampire, Swarmform patches the Vampire shape group and replaces its four animal choices, not its gaseous profile. Movement-triggered incorporeality, Wind Walk-dependent behavior, and feature suppression are `contextualModes`, not independently selectable forms. Ordinary spell grants, lasting transformations, and fixed creature traits are not split into form profiles.

`effects.stateChangesById` uses the same transition format for rage, progressive fatigue, cumulative fear, and reference-only Vampire sunlight. `tools.state_changes` applies transitions to caller-owned state; the caller supplies rage eligibility and expiry, rest, and encounter events, and passes the resulting condition IDs to sheet derivation. Fear durations and recovery, rage use limits and combat modifiers, and Vampire exposure detection and turn enforcement are not managed by this helper. The sunlight profile is not automatically attached to any creature or form.

`environmentStatesById` holds independent, toggleable environment profiles. Each profile uses the core transition shape; store its current state per actor and send `exposure-on` / `exposure-off` when the table resolves the exposure. Round, hour, and day events are explicit; the catalogue does not start a clock. A `resolve-source-effect` event points back to the original prose for adjudication, not executable damage or healing. This does not add profiles to core or attach them to play automatically. Complex conditions (such as Shadow Blend availability and whether water shields a Drowned One) are resolved by the caller before toggling. Daylight spell exposure and natural sunlight are separate facts.

### Named condition rules

All 38 named conditions now have source-linked typed mechanics in `effects.conditions[].mechanics`, mirrored in `effects.sheetConditionRulesById`. Descriptions remain reference text, not a second mechanics source. Sheet calculations consume flags, typed modifiers, implied conditions, severity, and effective scores. Turn budgets consume no-action, attention-only, single-move, and single-move-or-standard flags when supplied at turn start.

Typed operations are not necessarily automated. The caller still resolves things like wind displacement, confusion dice and targets, compelled flight, mental-action eligibility, casting checks, stabilization and recovery, and coup de grace. Source-dependent values (such as Checked's flying displacement), long-term sensory adaptation, and DM calls on strenuous actions remain explicit rather than receiving invented defaults.

### Encounter and drain integration

- `tools.encounter_conditions.change_conditions` applies condition events and recalculates the active turn budget without refunding spent actions. Use `advance_turn` for subsequent turns and pass `effective_conditions` to the sheet. End rage with `tirelessRage` supplied; send encounter-end and rest events when the table confirms them. DM rulings can skip a transition, replace its state or the condition list, or override turn flags; the reason is recorded in the encounter. An empty flag override removes default turn restrictions, and `null` releases that override. Positional and forced-behavior decisions still belong to the encounter caller.
- `tools.spellcasting.recover_spell_tracks` refreshes a batch finishing together and reapplies active negative-level slot losses. The caller supplies current equipment-based levels and the player's choices for ties. `tools.drain_history` commits timed levels, resolves individual due saves, and projects permanent losses over the original level history; Restoration reactivates eligible levels and records the restoration. Drain deaths are flagged, with next-night aftermath reported for creature-caused lethal acquisition. Consumers must use the projected level history and reconcile level-dependent HP, skill and feat selections, companions, and spell-slot budgets against it; this does not rewrite those independently stored character records. Re-advancement after a permanent loss is not yet reconciled with the preserved history.

Sourced conditions are instance overlays (`conditionOverrides`), each with its own ID, cause and optional expiry. Removing or suppressing an instance does not rewrite the base sheet. `effects.conditionCuresBySpellId` records confirmed cure scope and exceptions; use `cure_encounter_conditions` for encounter action refresh or `cure_actor` for an actor's condition, ability-loss and drain ledgers. Target legality, spell resistance, original-source eligibility for curse/enchantment removal, and rolls are caller inputs; suppression is not removal. The catalogue does not yet model every indirect spell interaction (such as Freedom of Movement bypassing restrictions without curing them). Negative levels can be removed one at a time, and permanent drain/Restoration write `drainXpOverride`, not base XP. Consumers should read `effective_xp` when they need the current XP total.

An active negative level does not change class levels or purchased skill ranks. Its skill penalty applies once to the check, independently of the effective-level, attack, save, ability-check, HP and spell losses. Permanent level loss after a failed drain save is a separate event, not an effect of an active negative level.

### Injury workflow

`mechanics.injury` and `tools.injury` resolve already-rolled damage, DR bypass, round-scoped DR and resistance, temporary HP, injury conditions, first aid and healing. Give each encounter round a unique ID and use the returned injury state for subsequent hits; resistance is tracked separately per energy type and DR uses a shared nonstacking allowance. Massive damage is checked after those defenses but before temporary HP. Call `end_injury_round` once at round end with the dying actor's percentile roll. Pass returned condition IDs to encounter/sheet state. Critical roll generation and temporary HP grant stacking are still caller-owned.

### Alignment spell automation boundary

For alignment spells, automate numeric modifiers on the recipient's sheet only, with their existing alignment and stacking requirements. Preserve the full spell description for player and DM adjudication of effects beyond the recipient or effects that do not change sheet numbers. In particular, do not automatically resolve mental-control suppression, summoned-creature contact, Magic Circle area or trap behavior, or offensive target effects as part of recipient-sheet automation. Existing typed reference data can remain available for consultation; it does not authorize automatic application. Alignment filters and spell effects are unchanged.

### All-effects automation audit

The recipient-sheet boundary applies to all effects, not just alignment spells. `effects.applicationPolicy` declares a fail-closed default; spell, feat and condition modifier catalogues explicitly mark `recipient-sheet` or `adjudicate`. A die, DC, range, or duration in an effect does not make it a sheet modifier. Condition action restrictions, spell cures, forced movement, and targeting or area rules remain descriptive or typed references; existing explicit resolution helpers are available after the player and DM adjudicate the outcome. No type or reference grants an effect by itself.

`tools/audit_core.py` checks that the global policy is present, that tagged effects agree with their scope, and that spells, conditions, feats, class abilities, special abilities, race traits, diseases, poisons, rules, equipment, and creatures retain adjudication text or a cited table/source. This is a structural audit, not a claim of clause-by-clause semantic parity with every source.

### TXT comparison of adjudication prose

Run `python tools/source_audit.py` for the record-level report. It compares both descriptions of all 611 spells, all 38 condition descriptions, and all 124 feat bodies against their source blocks, normalizing whitespace, case and typographic marks. It also checks 301 class-ability descriptions, 163 race traits, and 213 special-ability paragraphs against their cited source files. Spell and condition descriptions are now refreshed by generation rather than inherited from the old JSON snapshot. This restores the complete baseline spell text, including inline exceptions and cross-references, and the current Protection from Energy wording.

Fifteen class descriptions did not match a contiguous source passage. Review the exact IDs in the audit output before changing their typed counterparts; a mismatch can mean an edited paraphrase or a description assembled from several paragraphs. This prose audit does not establish equivalence of every numeric rule, table, or typed execution path. Inventory, companions, diseases, poisons, and general rules still have the separate structural source-retention checks.

### Locked gauntlets

For an equipped `armor:gauntlet-locked` instance, set `hand` to `primary` or `offhand` and set `securedWeaponInstanceId` only after the table confirms the weapon is attached. The sheet exposes the +10 disarm defense bonus on that weapon only and corrects the listed carried weight when the gauntlet replaces part of fitted armor. Include the catalog's 5 lb per gauntlet in the input carried weight; the sheet subtracts it for replacements.

The sheet returns both the source description and a player/DM adjudication note. Attaching or detaching takes a full-round action and provokes attacks of opportunity. A locked hand cannot be used to cast spells or employ skills, but a free other hand can supply somatic components. The equipment table's 100% entry is preserved as reference, not applied as blanket spell failure. Resolve forced drops at the table when a weapon is secured. A gauntlet unarmed strike may deal lethal damage instead of nonlethal; the player chooses and the DM resolves the attack. None of these actions or choices is executed by deriving the sheet.

### Arcane spell failure from armor

Pass the confirmed worn armor and shield in use to `plan_cast` as `worn_armor_and_shields` (item IDs). `armorSpellFailure` reports their contributions and the final percentage for that casting track, after metamagic changes the components. Only arcane spells with a somatic component count. Bard light-armor casting waives the armor contribution only; it does not waive shield failure. The locked gauntlet remains a hand restriction, not a percentage contribution.

When a cast completes with a nonzero chance, do not apply its effects until the table resolves the percentile roll. `resolve_armor_spell_failure(plan, roll)` accepts that roll and returns no applicable spell on failure. The cast already spent its action and slot; neither is refunded. No die is rolled automatically.
